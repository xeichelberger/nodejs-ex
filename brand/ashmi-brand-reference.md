## What We Sell

Ashmi & Co. is a premium baby and toddler clothing brand for ages 0 to 24 months. We design pieces that are photogenic, occasion-ready, and built to last through hand-me-downs. Our clothing is organized by occasion, not by age. The two main categories are EVERYDAY (pieces that make ordinary days feel intentional) and SPECIAL (pieces designed for milestones, holidays, photos, and celebrations).

### Product Categories

- Rompers (our bread and butter, including flutter sleeve styles)
- Dresses
- Sets (matching top and bottom combos, including jacquard sets)
- Overalls
- Leggings
- Joggers
- Outerwear and layering pieces
- Turbans and headband accessories
- Matching sibling sets

### Price Range

- Accessories start around $15 (headbands, turbans)
- Core pieces sit between $42 and $58
- Premium pieces (jacquard sets, special occasion) go up to $80
- Average order value is approximately $60

### Design Aesthetic

The brand leans into warm, feminine tones for girls (dusty rose, sage, cream, lavender) and earthy neutrals for boys (khaki, olive, navy). Design details include flutter sleeves, ruffles, jacquard textures, knit patterns, and button-front closures. The overall look is polished and occasion-ready rather than basic or trendy. Think elevated, timeless, photograph-worthy.

We are not cartoon prints. We are not loud graphics. We are not fast fashion basics. We are not novelty or trend-chasing.

### Fabric and Quality

Our pieces use premium cotton and high-quality fabrics. Customer reviews consistently praise the softness, structure, color richness, and durability. We design pieces with enough substance to feel intentional but enough softness to be genuinely comfortable for a baby who needs to crawl, eat, nap, and move. The weight and quality of our fabrics is what separates us from mass-market alternatives.

**Note for content creation:** we describe our fabrics in specific, tactile terms. Instead of just saying "premium fabrics," describe what the cotton actually feels like. Thick enough to feel substantial. Soft enough that you notice it. Colors that hold through wash after wash.

## Who Our Customer Is

### Primary Customer: The DTC Buyer

**Demographics:** Female, 25 to 38 years old. Millennial and older Gen Z mothers. Upper middle income household (HHI $75K to $200K). Shops online regularly. Comfortable spending $40 to $80 on a special baby outfit. Active on Instagram and Pinterest.

**Psychographics:** She values aesthetics and intentional living. She believes early childhood is sacred and fleeting. She is drawn to neutral palettes and quiet luxury. She wants her baby to look polished but comfortable. She feels pride in thoughtful, curated choices. She avoids loud, overstimulating baby clothing. She photographs milestones. She is influenced by social proof and peer recommendations. She uses words like "intentional" to describe her own life.

**What she is buying emotionally:** "I want my baby to look as beautiful and intentional as this season of life feels."

**Where she hangs out:** Instagram, Pinterest, parenting groups, baby registry platforms, Nordstrom.com

### Secondary Customer: The Nordstrom Buyer

Similar age range to the DTC buyer but may discover us while browsing Nordstrom rather than seeking us out. She trusts Nordstrom's curation and uses it as a quality filter. She buys for her own baby and for gifting (baby showers, first holidays). She is less brand-loyal initially but open to discovering premium emerging brands.

**What she is buying emotionally:** "I want something elevated and beautiful that feels worth gifting or dressing my baby in."

### Tertiary Customers

**Gift buyers:** Grandparents, aunts, friends attending baby showers. They want something that looks and feels special without knowing the baby's exact size or preference. Our pieces read as "thoughtful gift" not "random onesie."

**Boutique retailers:** Shop owners seeking unique wholesale brands with strong design and Instagram appeal.

### Anti-Persona (Who We Are NOT For)

- Parents seeking the cheapest possible baby clothing
- Shoppers who want loud, novelty-driven prints or cartoon characters
- Heavy discount shoppers who only buy on sale
- Anyone looking for mass-market basics

## Brand Voice and Tone

### How We Sound

- **Calm.** Our reader is probably reading while nursing or during a rare quiet moment. The writing should feel like a deep breath.
- **Intentional.** Every sentence earns its place.
- **Warm.** Luxury does not mean cold or distant. We use "you" and "your." We share from experience.
- **Elevated.** We say "fleeting" instead of "short" and "silhouette" instead of "shape." But we never sound like we swallowed a thesaurus.
- **Grounded.** We always come back to the real baby who will wear this. The real mom reading at midnight.
- **Sentimental without being saccharine.** We acknowledge that babyhood is bittersweet and fast. We do not perform emotion or manufacture urgency.

### How We Sound in Practice

- "These early days deserve to be dressed beautifully."
- "She was wearing the dusty rose romper the first time she pulled herself up to standing."
- "The nursery looked this good for exactly one photograph, and then we actually brought a baby home."

### How We Do NOT Sound

- "Limited time! Grab this cute baby fit before it's gone!"
- "OMG these are SO adorable, mama! Your little one NEEDS this!"
- "Our premium garments are meticulously crafted to provide optimal comfort."

### Language We Use

- "These early days" or "this season" (not "this phase")
- "Beautiful" and "beauty" (our core word, used deliberately)
- "Intentional"
- "Quiet" as a modifier: quiet luxury, quiet confidence, quiet mornings
- "Soft" as both literal and emotional
- "Kept" and "heirloom" (things worth holding onto)
- "Elevated"
- "Worth it"
- First person when sharing experience: "I remember..." or "when my daughter was..."

### Language We Never Use

- "Mama" or "hey mama" or "mama bear"
- "Adorable," "cute," "precious" (everyone's words, not ours)
- "Obsessed" or "game changer" or "must-have" or "essentials"
- "Slay," "vibes," "aesthetic" as standalone words
- "Invest in" when talking about baby clothes
- "Journey" unless it truly fits
- "Curated" (we say "chosen with care" or "put together thoughtfully")
- "Elevate your..." (we show elevation, we do not promise it as a verb)
- Baby talk of any kind: "littles," "bubs," "mini me," "tiny human"

## SEO Content Guidelines

- Write for humans first and optimize for search second. Never reverse that priority.
- Use natural keyword phrases in the first paragraph when they fit organically.
- Write titles that a person would actually click on, not keyword salad.
- Answer real questions that real parents ask (long-tail keywords worth targeting).
- Internal link to products and collections when genuinely relevant.
- Keep meta descriptions conversational, not robotic.

**Good title example:** "What Nobody Tells You About Dressing a Baby for a Wedding"
**Bad title example:** "Complete Guide to Baby Wedding Outfits: Best Luxury Baby Clothes for Special Occasions 2025"
